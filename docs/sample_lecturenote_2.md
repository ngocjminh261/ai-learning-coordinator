Topic: Data Visualization Design Principles
Note:
Data visualization is the process of turning data into charts, graphs, and visual summaries so people can understand patterns more quickly.

A good visualization should make the main message easy to see. The chart type should match the data and the question being asked.

For comparing categories, bar charts are often a good choice because the audience can compare bar lengths easily.

For showing the distribution of one numeric variable, histograms are useful because they show how values are spread across ranges.

For showing the relationship between two numeric variables, scatter plots are useful because they can reveal patterns, clusters, and outliers.

For showing change over time, line charts are often useful because they make trends and movement easy to follow.

Design choices affect how people interpret a chart. Titles should describe what the chart is showing. Axis labels should be clear. Scales should be readable and not misleading.

Color should be used with purpose. It can highlight an important group, separate categories, or show intensity. Too many colors can make a chart harder to understand.

Charts should avoid unnecessary decoration. Extra 3D effects, heavy gridlines, and visual clutter can distract from the data.

One common mistake is using a chart that hides important variation. For example, showing only an average can hide outliers or skewed distributions.

In Exploratory Data Analysis, visualization is used to investigate the data, not just present final results. Analysts often create several charts to compare different views of the same dataset.

The goal of visualization is not just to make data look nice. The goal is to help people notice patterns, ask better questions, and make more informed decisions.
